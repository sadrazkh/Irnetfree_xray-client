# v1.27.0 — Groups: several configs used as one

## What the owner asked for

"Connections to one IP are sometimes limited — can several configs be connected at once, for more download and
upload speed?" Then: "CDN configs that all reach one server, through different CDN IPs". Then: "start what you
propose — optional: I make a config, add several to it, it becomes one config, and I use it in routing, advanced
routing and chains".

## What it does

- **Groups** («گروه», on the page now called «زنجیره و گروه» / Chains & groups): several configs used as one.
  Each new connection goes out through one member; a member that stops answering is left out until it answers
  again (a health check every 30 s, through every member).
  - **Spread over all** (default) — round-robin: more total speed when an app opens many connections (download
    managers, Telegram, a browser, speed tests). One download over one connection is no faster.
  - **Fastest** — everything through the lowest-latency live member, the others as spares.
  - **Random**.
- **One config, many clean IPs**: «+ IP تمیز» takes a config and a list of addresses (`ip`, `ip:port`, `[v6]:port`,
  a name) and makes one member per line — the same config dialled at that address, its SNI and Host kept on the
  original name. For CDN configs that reach one server this is the best case: the exit IP stays the same, so
  sites that tie a login to an IP see nothing change.
- **Used wherever a config is**: selected on the home page, a target in advanced routing (rules and default), a
  routing profile's base, a pool entry, any hop of a chain. Cards show the members (each with its own address, a
  removed one marked), real and upload delay, usage; the backup carries groups.
- **The traffic meter and usage count a group** as one config.

## Underneath

- `src/main/groups.js`: the stored group `{ id: grp-…, name, strategy, members: [{ id, address?, port? }] }` and the
  record a plan carries — a `loopback` outbound marked `_group` with its member records. main.js and the router put
  the records beside the servers, so a group id is a selection, a target, a base, a pool entry and a chain member.
  `src/main/addressSwap.js` (ported from Plus's scanner) dials a member at its own address.
- `configBuilder.expandGroups`: each group outbound T becomes its members `T~gm<i>`, the loopback `T` (inbound
  `T~in`), the balancer `T~bal` over the `T~gm` prefix with the first member as its fallback (Xray's round-robin and
  random skip dead members only when a fallback is set — app/router/balancing.go), the rule `T~in → T~bal` FIRST in
  routing, and one observatory over every group's members.
- **Nothing dials through a group.** A next hop whose dialerProxy named a group's loopback lost the end of a
  response about one run in four on both cores, while a loopback as a route's target never did. So a chain with a
  group in it is a group of whole chains — one variant per member in its place (every combination for two groups,
  at most 64) — and a group as a base makes each target through it the chain [group → target].
- The core counts a group's bytes on its members, never on the loopback: `stats.foldGroups` adds `T~gm<i>` into `T`.
- What the machine dials itself (TUN bypass, names resolved before the connect, WireGuard endpoints and resolvers)
  sees the members. A group runs on Xray (Xray-PattN when a member asks for it), never on sing-box.
- `scripts/probe-group.js` (CI, both suggested cores; locally on 26.3.27 and PattN 26.9.9, three runs): the app's
  own builder on real cores — the group selected, an advanced-routing target, a chain's first hop and its exit;
  round-robin across two live members, a dead one left out, the meter reading the traffic.
- `scripts/serve-renderer.js` was already 127.0.0.1-only (v1.26.0).
