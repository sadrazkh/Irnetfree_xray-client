# v1.23.0 — A WireGuard base and its own exit share one session

## What was wrong

1. **A WireGuard base that was also "exit at the base" fought itself.** A routing profile whose base is a WireGuard
   (a server, or a chain that ends in one) and that sends some traffic out at the base got the WireGuard twice in
   its config: once as the base the other targets ride on, once as the exit. That is two sessions on one key. The
   peer keeps only the newest, so each handshake cut the other line off. Both lines dropped in turn.
   - The same happened when the base itself was picked as a target, or when the default was "exit at the base".
   - Measured against a real WireGuard peer, the old config failed in about half of the requests on each line:
     - a server base: 4 of 10 at the base, 6 of 10 through it;
     - a chain base: 2 of 8 at the base, 5 of 8 through it.
2. **Android connected without a gone base.** Say a profile had a rule at its base, and the base was then deleted or
   replaced by a subscription update. The desktop and the router refused to connect and said why. Android connected,
   dropped that rule, and sent its traffic to the default. With a "direct" default, that traffic left with the real
   address.
3. **sing-box's suggested version (⭐) was still 1.13.14**, while a download had installed 1.14.3 since v1.21.1.

## The fix

1. **One WireGuard session.** When a base carries a WireGuard and is also dialled as a target, that target's outbound
   is a freedom that dials through the base's own outbound.
   - Its tag (`out-<id>`, `out-chain-<cid>`) stays the same. The home flow and the usage totals still show its own
     traffic, apart from the base's.
   - The corporate resolver still goes through that one session.
   - Only this case changes. Built with the old and the new builder, the other 10 sample plans came out identical
     byte for byte, the owner's corporate chain among them; only the 4 plans with the bug changed.
   - Android does the same (`ConfigBuilder.kt`).
2. **Android refuses, as the desktop does.** `RoutingProfiles.planRoutes` checks first that the base still exists,
   then gives the desktop's message. The rule editor says connecting is refused, not that the rule is skipped.
3. **⭐ sing-box 1.14.3**, GitHub's latest stable.
   - It was already verified on a real TUN in CI (the `tun-dns` job) and is now also the version the `cores` job
     checks every TUN config with.
   - 1.13.14 stays verified, and the macOS app still bundles it: its TUN config is its own, not `tunSingbox.js`'s.
   - A version older than the ⭐ now asks first in the picker. Nothing installs by itself: a download already
     installed 1.14.3.

## How it was verified

- **`scripts/probe-wg-base.js`** (new; `npm run probe:wg-base`) uses the app's own builder against a real WireGuard
  peer (sing-box), all on loopback. It runs with the base as a server, then as a chain ending in the WireGuard. Each
  run checks:
  - "exit at the base", by names that only the corporate DNS resolves inside the tunnel;
  - a hop through the base;
  - 2 MB four at once, two on each line;
  - the exit's own traffic figure;
  - exactly one WireGuard outbound.
- Results so far:
  - On this machine with Xray 26.3.27 and 25.8.3: 10 of 10 on both lines, in both runs.
  - On the old builder the same probe fails both runs.
  - CI runs it on the suggested Xray 26.9.30 and Xray-PattN 26.9.22.
- The corporate-chain probe (`scripts/probe-wg-chain.js`) still passes: intranet name resolved, HTTP and HTTPS,
  2 MB each.
- `validate-configs.js` adds both shapes, under managed DNS, TUN and the strict guard. All 333 configs are accepted.

## Unchanged

Every config without a WireGuard base that is also dialled as a target, byte for byte.
