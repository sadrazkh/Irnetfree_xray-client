By the version rule: fixes to what already exists, so the third number goes up.

## The subscription: why it jumped

Everything the app keeps about a server points at it by id: the selection, a chain's hops, a routing rule and its
default, a group's members, a pool entry. A refresh parses the list again and has to find each server's old id. It looked
for the same link, or the same address, credential and path.

His two panels **rotate**. On every fetch they change the address, Host, SNI and path, and in one of them part of the
names, all under one uuid for the whole list. Two fetches seconds apart:

| Panel | Configs | Same identity in both | Ids kept by a refresh, before |
|---|---|---|---|
| JSON | 38 | 5 | 8 |
| Links | 19 | 9 | 7 |

So every refresh gave most servers new ids. His own store had the proof: a chain's hop, a routing default and all four
members of a group pointed at ids that no longer existed. The VPN going off did not cause it: the hourly refresh had moved
the ids under a live tunnel, and the picker is what he looks at when it goes off.

## The fix

A server is now found in this order, each pass over what is still unmatched, on the desktop, the router and Android:

1. the identical link;
2. the link without its remark, under the same country and the same name apart from its numbers, so a panel that renumbers
   its list when one server leaves is decided by its links;
3. the panel's name, where one server has it on each side;
4. the same country, and the link without its remark, then what it connects to;
5. the name, in order; then the name apart from its numbers on the same account;
6. the same country on the same account, in order: a panel's slots for a country outlast what it calls them;
7. what it connects to, where only one side has a flag: a panel that started writing them.

Two flags that disagree are never one server, so a chain does not quietly move to another country. Without a flag, a
server that left and one that came under another name are not taken for each other. The name compared is the panel's, so
a name the user gave a server does not hide it, JSON servers included. A clean IP the user put on a server survives the
rotation.

Replaying six real fetches in a row through the new code: 19 of 19 and 38 of 38 ids kept, none in another country.

Also: a learnt certificate pin stays only while the address, port and SNI are the ones it was learnt on, and is learnt again
otherwise. A selected server the panel really dropped falls back to its own subscription.

**Not recovered:** references already dangling, because the ids they name are gone. Those are picked once more, and hold
from then on.

## Android: why it took a Force Stop

Every tunnel start and stop runs on one worker thread, one after another, and the calls there are native and cannot be
interrupted. hev 2.16.0's stop spins for ever after a start whose init failed (read in its source), holding its mutex; the
in-process core's StartLoop and StopLoop can block in Go. One such call froze everything behind it: the Disconnect, the
Cancel, the next Connect. The screen kept its word, the notification stayed, the TUN stayed open with every app routed into
it, and only killing the process ended it.

- **Nothing holds the worker for good.** hev's stop waits 8 s, the core's stop 10 s, the core's start 30 s, each on a thread
  of its own. The teardown goes on past them: the TUN closes, Not connected shows, the service stops.
- **A slow call is not a stuck one.** One that returns late clears its mark, and a start that returns late has its orphan
  core stopped. A connect waits a few seconds for that before refusing, so a slow stop on a server switch reconnects.
- **A process really stuck runs no tunnel again**, and restarts itself: at once when nobody tapped (always-on, a rebuild),
  and otherwise when every screen of ours is away, or on the second tap, which the screen announces first.
- **Never against the user's word.** A Disconnect asked for during a stuck start is honoured, not reversed by a reconnect.
- **No loop.** Two self-reconnects in ten minutes at most; the next stays off and a notification says why.
- **A test core's hang leaves a healthy tunnel alone**, ⚡ and the mux probe included.
- **The tap answers at once.** "Disconnecting…" on Home and in the notification, where "Connected" used to stay up while the
  worker finished a start. People reach for Force Stop within seconds of a dead button.
- The wedge clock is uptime, so a phone waking from deep sleep is not taken for a stuck command.

## Limits and what is next

- Bumping hev (2.17+ has `TProxyIsRunning` and a stop that cannot spin) is the upstream fix for its part. Its JNI table
  changes, and that cannot be tried without a device, so it is not in this patch.
- A config whose hev init fails still shows Connected until the self-check says no packets entered the TUN. Same reason.
- A self-restart on a tap closes the screen: the user reopens the app, and the VPN reconnects by itself. The text says so.
