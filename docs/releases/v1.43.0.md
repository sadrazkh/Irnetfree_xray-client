# v1.43.0 — Traffic by site

By the version rule: a new capability, so the second number goes up.

## What the owner asked

An option in the main client to see roughly where his traffic goes: "do I use YouTube more, or Instagram, or …".

## What is new

- **Logs → Traffic by site** (src/main/siteUsage.js, src/renderer/siteUsage.js): a switch, four periods (today, 7 days,
  30 days, all), and a row per service or site, biggest first, with its share and bytes. Download and upload are in the
  row's tooltip, and a button clears the figures.
- **Known services are one row** (YouTube is youtube.com, googlevideo.com, ytimg.com and more). Any other site is a row
  under its registrable domain.
- **No name:** Telegram's apps dial bare addresses, so its published ranges count as Telegram. Anything else without a
  name goes under its program, and the rest under Other.
- **Off by default, and a reconnect key.** Off, the TUN's config is byte-identical to before.
- **Local only:** saved per day in usage.json for at most 92 days, the registrable domain and never a full host. Nothing
  leaves the machine.

## How it works

Only sing-box's TUN can say. Xray counts per outbound and per user, never per name, and its access log has names
without bytes. The app already reads sing-box's loopback API for the Connections tab; it lists every open connection
with its bytes.

The API has a name only if sing-box sniffs, which the TUN did not do. With the option on, a connect adds a first route
rule `{ action: 'sniff' }` (the inbound's `sniff` before sing-box 1.11). It only reads the name: the destination stays
the address, so what the core is asked to dial does not change. A server-first protocol can wait up to sing-box's
sniff timeout (300 ms) on a new connection while the option is on.

The meter reads every second: each connection's bytes since the last read go to its row. What the API's running totals
moved that no listed connection explains (a connection that opened and closed between two reads, the last bytes of one
that ended) goes to Other, so the sum is what moved.

## Limits

- **TUN on sing-box only.** Not system-proxy mode, tun2socks or the macOS service; the tab says why and what to change.
- **Not the phone or the router.** Android's tunnel (hev + Xray) has no per-connection bytes or names to read; on the
  router the tab is hidden.
- **Approximate:** payload as the apps see it, without the VPN's overhead. Facebook and Instagram share a CDN
  (fbcdn.net), which counts as Facebook.
- **Advanced mode:** the Logs page is pro-only, as before.

## Tested

- `npm test`: tests/siteUsage.test.js.
- **scripts/probe-site-usage.js** in CI, a real sing-box running the app's own TUN route on a mixed inbound, read by the
  app's own meter: a TLS page under www.youtube.com lands in YouTube with its bytes, one under files.aparat.com under
  aparat.com, and the totals match. Without the sniff (the control) neither has a name.
- **scripts/tun-dns-probe.js**, the real TUN in a namespace: sing-box reads the name from a TLS hello to a bare address.
- **The cores gate:** `sing-box check` of the sniff rule ahead of a per-app list.

## Owed

The owner's own use: turn it on, reconnect, watch YouTube and Instagram for a while, and see whether the rows and the
size of Other look right. How big Other gets on real browsing has not been measured.
