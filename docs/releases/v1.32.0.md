# v1.32.0 — multi-internet: two connected internets used together

By the version rule: a new capability — the second number.

## What the owner asked for

«یه بخش برای مرج کردن اینترنتا … دو تا اینترنت وصله یکیش اپلود خوبی داره یکیش دانلود خوب بشه با ترکیبشون سرعت و
پایداری رو برد بالا یا لیمت گذاشت … وایفای این محدودیت این حجم». His choices: desktop (Windows/macOS/Linux) and the
router (OpenWrt); balance + failover, split upload/download, data cap and share per internet. Built with four parallel
agents against one contract (core transform, link discovery + speed test, UI/IPC/caps, a CI probe on real links).

## What it does

- **Settings → Multi-internet** (فارسی: ترکیب اینترنت‌ها): every connected internet the OS has a default route for
  (Wi-Fi, Ethernet, USB tethering, a 4G dongle; on the router each WAN), with its address and gateway.
- **Balance** — each new connection on the next internet in turn, by its share (1×/2×/3×); a backup internet joins
  only when the others are down; an internet that stops answering is skipped within ~10 s.
- **Failover** — everything on the first internet; the next one only while the first is down; back when it returns.
- **Split upload/download** — for XHTTP servers (packet-up/stream-up): uploads leave by one internet and downloads
  arrive on the other, even within one connection (XHTTP's downloadSettings with its own interface). Other servers are
  balanced over the two.
- **Data caps** — per internet, per day or per month (GB): counted from the core's own per-outbound counters; at the cap
  the internet is set aside (one reload) and back in the next period; the usage bar shows used/cap and the period end.
- **Speed test of each internet** — RTT, download and upload through the server, one internet at a time.
- What it cannot do, said in the UI: one download is never faster than its own internet (that needs the server to
  combine — MPTCP — which the app does not do); chains and WireGuard servers keep dialling by the OS's default route.

## Underneath

- src/main/multiWan.js: a server that dials by itself becomes a roundRobin group of copies bound to each internet
  (`sockopt.interface`; a share = copies; a backup = the group's fallback), failover = one member plus a fallback,
  split = the upload's interface + `xhttpSettings.extra.downloadSettings` with the other. Groups are multiplied per
  internet. The dpi dialers carry the interface of what dials through them; bindDirectDials leaves a bound outbound
  alone. Off, every config is byte-identical (pinned).
- src/main/netLinks.js: the internets on Windows (Get-NetRoute + adapters), macOS (netstat + networksetup), Linux and
  OpenWrt (ip route/addr + ubus); the speed test through a throwaway core bound to one.
- src/main/multiWanMeter.js: usage per internet per period (in usage.json, written at most every 30 s), the cap's
  set-aside and return; multiwan:get/set/test on desktop and router; src/renderer/multiWan.js: the card.

## Proven

- CI `multiwan` job (scripts/probe-multiwan.js): two real veth links between network namespaces, the app's own
  buildConfig, both suggested cores. Balance: 8 parallel downloads 50 % / 50 % over the two 100 Mbit links, 194 Mbit/s
  together. Failover: the primary cut → the other within ~12 s; back ~9 s after it returns. Split: a 20 MB upload by one
  link, a 20 MB download by the other, both ways round. multiWan off: byte-identical, and everything on the OS's link.
- The cores' `-test` over 18 multi-internet configs (plain and under TUN). npm test: the new suites (multiWan,
  netLinks, multiWanMeter, multiWanIpc, multiWanUi, multiWanProbe).

## Not proven here

On the owner's machines: two real internets at once (Windows with Wi-Fi + USB tethering; the router with two WANs —
each WAN needs its own route metric there), the caps over a real day/month, the speed test against Cloudflare.
