# v1.45.4 — A site on a WireGuard's address stays in the tunnel

By the version rule: a fix to something that already exists, so the third number goes up.

## What the owner reported

`rain.tes.ca` and `earth.tes.ca` would not open under advanced routing, "even though I never touched them", while a simple config opened them, and his WireGuard on the laptop stopped working behind the router. With the WireGuard not even on, `ping rain.tes.ca` got "Destination net unreachable" from `192.168.2.1` while `cloud.tes.ca`, one address away, was fine. He had said it from the start: "it leaves by another way, not the route I built".

## The cause

`rain`, `earth` and `cobra.tes.ca` are one address, the machine of the corporate WireGuard.

`wgEndpointHosts` resolves the endpoint of every stored WireGuard in an advanced plan, because `serversById` holds every server. Every answer then went into `pinnedIps`, the addresses kept off the tunnel. So under any advanced plan that address left outside the VPN: a WireGuard with no rule at all, or one that rides a base and is never dialled from here. A simple plan has no `serversById`, which is why it worked there and only there.

`wgEndpointHosts` has walked every server since v0.14.2 (2026-09-13), and `pinnedIps` arrived in v1.14.0 (2026-09-24). It stayed out of sight while that address could be reached directly. **Not established:** why the direct way stopped now.

**Why it took this long:** every test on a throwaway core ran without a TUN, where no bypass exists, and masking addresses in the output hid that the site and the WireGuard shared one.

## What changed

`pinnedEntryIps`: the bypass holds a WireGuard's address only when the core dials it itself: an entry of the plan, or a WireGuard outbound with no hop in front of it (a noise dialer counts as none). Desktop and router.

**The core's config does not change.** Chains, bases, advanced rules and the corporate WireGuard on a base are written as before; only the list handed to the tunnel is shorter. A WireGuard dialled directly stays out of the tunnel as it must, or it would loop into it.

## Tested

`tests/wgBypass.test.js`: the chained WireGuard's config as it was, unused and on a base inside the tunnel, direct and single and first hop out of it, a later chain hop inside it, the router's service end to end.

## Limits and owed

- **A WireGuard dialled directly (`via none`)** still takes the sites on its address with it. Put it on a base.
- **Not run on a real router or under a real TUN here.** The test that says it: `ping 51.222.52.23` behind the router no longer answers "net unreachable".
