# v1.39.0 — limits by kind, routing, hours and speeds per user

By the version rule: a new capability, so the second number goes up.

## What the owner asked

«ترافیک متفاوت گذاشت برای دایرکت و استفاده از وی پی ان … شبیه اون روتینگه برای یوزرها هم جدا بشه گذاشت … یه کلی و
لیمیت هم بشه روزانه ماهانه و ترکیبی باشه … بعدش چیزایی که بنظرت کاربردین خودت بگو», and then «لیمیت پهنای باند هم
… ساعت های پرکار … 2 گیگ ماکزیمم سرعت بقیش … فلان حجم فلان سرعت دانلود اپلود».

His choices:
- When a VPN limit is reached, only the VPN stops and direct keeps working.
- Per-user routing is the simple modes plus his routing profiles.
- All four extras I suggested: 80/100 % warnings, a top-up and a reset, allowed hours, and a 30-day chart.

## What a user now has

- **Limits** (model.js `limits`):
  - **kinds and periods:** total, VPN or direct, each daily, monthly (the user's calendar and renew day) or
    one-off, in any combination;
  - **what happens when one is over:** it either cuts or slows to a down/up rate ("2 GB at full speed, then
    slower");
  - **compatibility:** `quotaGb/quotaPeriod` stay as a mirror of the first total limit, for older readers.
- **Routing** (`routing`):
  - `''` = as before. On the desktop's accounts that means everything through the user's config; on the router it
    means the router's own routing.
  - `bypass-ir` = private ranges and Iranian sites direct.
  - `profile:<id>` = one of the app's routing profiles, with the user's config as its default.
- **Allowed hours** (`schedule`): the days of the week and a window, which may run overnight.
- **Speeds** (`speed`, `speedHours`), router only: a base down/up rate, up to 4 time windows with their own
  rates, and a slow limit's rate once it is over. The strictest one applies.

## How it is counted

- **A direct outbound per user:** every user gets `lu-d-<id>`, a clone of the plan's own `direct`. Every pass binds
  it the same way: the NIC under a desktop TUN, the WAN on a router.
- **Direct bytes:** read per user from `stats.outbound`. VPN = total − direct.
- **On the router:** each user has a transparent inbound of their own (`lu-tp-<n>` per user, no longer per
  config). The router's own rules keep their order: a copy scoped to the user sits right before each one that ends
  direct, so it is counted as theirs. A router rule that blocks (ads), or sends a domain through a server, still
  decides first.
- **The meter** (meter.js, rewritten):
  - keeps day, month and one-off counts per kind, top-ups and the warnings (saved, never said twice), and 30 days
    of history;
  - **holds:** a hold is 'all', 'vpn' or 'direct', with why and until, and speeds go with it. On the router,
    routing '' has direct routes, so a VPN-over user there is held from the VPN only.
- **Speeds on the router:** nft policing per device in the users' forward chain, before the counters. Upload is
  policed by MAC, download by the device's addresses (one shared bucket per family). A change reloads the table
  atomically.
- **Reloads:** forBuild now knows the router's users (`routerTp`). A router user's partial hold, routing or config
  change reloads the core; before, it never did.

## Where it shows

- **The Users page:**
  - **in the user's dialog:** the 3×3 limits grid with cut or slow, the routing select (with the profiles), the
    allowed hours, and, on the router, speeds and peak-hour windows;
  - **on the user's card:** a bar per limit with what is left, hold badges (direct only, VPN only, outside hours)
    with until, Top up and Reset, the current speed and why, and a 30-day stacked VPN/direct chart.
- **The router's sign-in page:** used / cap / left per limit, the speed and why. It also says why a sign-in is
  refused and when that lifts: outside your allowed hours, or over a cap.
- **The home screen's live view:** folds `lu-d-*` into direct, so users' direct traffic is not shown as VPN speed.

## Proven

- `npm test`: 2925 pass, 9 skipped.
- **Unit tests added:**
  - the model's migration and validation;
  - the meter's kinds, periods, holds, top-up, reset, warnings and speeds;
  - the per-user rules (bypass-ir, profiles, holds) and `lu-d-*` equal to `direct` under a TUN;
  - the router per user (decide, the placements, nft rates byte for byte, the sign-in page's new texts);
  - the Users page (the grid round trip, routing, hours, bars, badges, top-up/reset, the chart, speeds).
- **The CI cores job, on the core and UI commit:**
  - validate-configs' 15 new shapes on the real cores;
  - probe-lan-users' new gates: a profile rule's target answered over direct, counted on `lu-d-<id>` and not on
    the server; under a VPN hold, direct works and the rest fails.
- **Headless renders** of the dialog and the cards, in fa and en.
- **Router pieces, proven by CI on this commit:**
  - the per-user and speed gates of the netns probe;
  - the QEMU guest's `nft -c` of a table with rates.

## Not proven

- On the owner's devices: the AC-1304, and real phones behind the router once his second routers are access
  points.
- Speed limits are policing (drops), not shaping: TCP backs off to them, but a download's rate shows sawteeth.
