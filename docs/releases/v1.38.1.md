# v1.38.1 — the router's sign-in page signs in from a real browser

By the version rule: a fix to something that already exists (the transparent mode's sign-in page) — the third
number.

## What the owner reported

«این هنوز مشکل داره وارد نمیشه با یوزر پسورد» — after v1.34.3, the router's sign-in page still refused him, with
«خطایی پیش آمد — دوباره امتحان کنید / Something went wrong». His browser's DevTools showed `POST /login → 403`
and the page's `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.

## The cause: the page's own header

The page was served with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. Under that policy Chrome sends the page's own form post
with `Origin: null`, and the portal treated any `Origin` other than its own as a form posted from another page.
It answered 403 before the username or password was read. So every sign-in from Chrome had failed since v1.33.0, and
v1.34.3's typing fold never came into play. Every test had posted with Node's http client, which sends whatever
headers the test gives it.

A real Chrome, driven against the portal on this machine, showed the exact page the owner saw: `Origin: null`,
`Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`, 403.

Now:
- The page sends `Referrer-Policy: same-origin`, so its own form carries its real Origin.
- The check reads the browser's `Sec-Fetch-Site` first: `same-origin` or `none` passes, anything else is refused. It
  falls back to `Origin` only when that header is missing.
- An `Origin` of `null` alone passes. A forged post could at most sign a device in or out, while a false refusal
  keeps the device off the internet.
- A refusal is logged with the two headers.

## https lands on the page too

In login mode, the gateway sent only an unassigned device's tcp/80 to the page. Its tcp/443 was refused, so an https
site said "can't be reached". Its tcp/443 now goes to the page's port as well. No page can stand in for another
site's https without a certificate warning, but plain HTTP answering a TLS hello fails at once as
`ERR_SSL_PROTOCOL_ERROR`. Browsers take that error as a sign-in page in the way: Chrome then checks, and opens the
page in a tab of its own. The device's system check (Windows, Android, iPhone) still opens it on joining the
network, over plain http.

## Proven

- `npm test` (2875 pass).
- tests/lanUsersPortalBrowser.test.js drives a headless Chrome, and runs on CI or with IRNF_RENDER_BROWSER:
  - the form, filled and submitted as a person would, signs in;
  - https to the page's port fails as ERR_SSL_PROTOCOL_ERROR;
  - run against v1.38.0's portal, it fails with the owner's page and `POST /login → 403`.
- lanUsersRouterPortal: the Chrome header shape signs in, while cross-site, same-site and cross-site-with-null are
  refused and logged.
- The netns probe has a new gate: the unassigned device's :443 lands on the login page.

## Not proven

- On the owner's AC-1304, and with Chrome's own captive-portal tab on a real network.
